### Simulation Suggestion 2

Title: Nutrient Movement Through a Villus

Learning objective: Explain absorption into the bloodstream through the small intestine.

Scientific model: An interactive cross-section of the small intestine wall.

Student goal: The user controls two sliders: "Wall Shape" (Flat to Highly Folded/Villi) and "Wall Thickness" (Thick to One Cell Thick).

Expected pattern: As the user adjusts the sliders to match the actual anatomy of the small intestine (Folded and One Cell Thick), animated nutrient particles cross the barrier into the blood much faster. A simple graph tracks the "Rate of Absorption". Accessibility: Ensure the sliders can be adjusted using arrow keys. Provide clear, high-contrast colours to distinguish the intestine interior from the bloodstream.


### Answer Key: Nutrient Movement Through a Villus Simulation

For the slider-adjustment interactive station, the correct optimal settings and their scientific reasoning are:
Wall Shape: Slide to Highly Folded (Villi).

Result: The rate of absorption increases on the graph.

Explanation: Folding the inner wall of the small intestine creates tiny structures called villi. This greatly increases the surface area. A larger surface area provides more space for soluble nutrients to pass into the blood at the same time.

Wall Thickness: Slide to One Cell Thick.

Result: The rate of absorption reaches its maximum speed.

Explanation: A wall that is only one cell thick creates a very short travel distance. This thin barrier allows nutrient particles to quickly and easily pass into the bloodstream.

Feedback Logic for Incorrect Choices
If the wall shape is left flat:
"Not quite! A flat wall gives a very small surface area. This means fewer nutrients can enter the blood at the same time. Try adding some folds (villi)!"
If the wall thickness is set to thick:
"Almost! A thick wall creates a very long journey for the nutrients to reach the blood. This slows down absorption. Try making the barrier much thinner!"
