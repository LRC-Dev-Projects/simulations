**Simulation Suggestion 2**

Title: The Energy Balance Scale

Learning Objective: Explain how diet affects health, growth, and physical activity.

Scientific Model: A dynamic seesaw scale balancing "Energy Intake" (Food) against "Energy Output" (Activity and Growth).

Starting State: A perfectly level scale. On the left side are blocks representing food (Carbs/Fats). On the right side are blocks representing activity.

Student Goal: Observe what happens when intake and output do not match, and make adjustments to restore balance.

Interaction: Students can click buttons to "Increase Activity" or "Add More Food". If they add more food without increasing activity, the scale tips heavily to the left, and a health warning appears. If they increase activity without adding food, the scale tips to the right, showing an "Exhaustion" warning.

Expected Pattern: Students learn that energy intake must proportionally match energy output to maintain a healthy balance.

Accessibility: Use high-contrast colours for the blocks. Ensure the state of the scale (balanced, tipped left, tipped right) is clearly described in a live-text box for screen readers.



**Simulation Answer Key**

Simulation Suggestion 2: The Energy Balance Scale

Expected Observations:

Adding food without increasing activity: The scale tips heavily to the side of Energy Intake. This shows a surplus of energy, which the body stores.

Increasing activity without adding food: The scale tips heavily to the side of Energy Output. This shows an energy deficit, which can lead to exhaustion or weight loss.

Matching food intake to activity level: The scale remains perfectly level. This represents a healthy energy balance.

Success Criteria: The student must successfully restore balance to the scale in each scenario by proportionally matching the food blocks to the activity blocks.

Correct Feedback: "Excellent observation! You have demonstrated that to maintain a healthy balance, the energy you take in from your diet must match the energy your body uses for daily activity and growth."
